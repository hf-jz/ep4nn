ep4nn: evidence-gated structural growth on a measured connectome

Hefei Jiuzhai Big Data Technology / Lituo Cloud Intelligence Technology (Nanjing)

2026-10-14

Abstract

We present ep4nn, a continuing network that begins as the measured 302-neuron C. elegans wiring imported into a rate-based equilibrium model and, when it meets two situations it cannot tell apart, appends neurons and synapses under three gates: a prerequisite gate (does the answer move at all), an evidence gate (one collision is noise), and an outcome gate (growth that does not reduce the collision is rolled back). The system is built on the released Cadence library and reuses two practices from the open fly-connectome projects: protocols written before their data, and receipts that separate nominal from executed scale. We report measurements rather than claims. On one laptop CPU the library settles a 10,000-neuron, 103,943-synapse graph in 6.5 ms and a 100,000-neuron, 1,239,921-synapse graph in 77.4 ms (32 sweeps, CSR transport, about 1.76 ns per synapse). A state-dependent contraction certificate exists on the worm graph where the library’s global worst-case bound fails (local spectral radius 0.529 against a worst-case row bound of 88.0). The growth machinery behaves as specified: over 1,200 ticks it refused growth 1,200 times because the prerequisite failed, and an earlier variant without the prerequisite gate produced 30 growths and 30 rollbacks. The paper’s main negative finding is that prerequisite: across 60 operating points of the worm graph the declared readout responds along a single direction (best spread 0.0777 with collision cosine 1.0), so capacity growth is not the right repair and ep4nn declines to grow. We conclude that structural growth studies must publish the prerequisite measurement alongside the growth result, and we propose a receipt format that makes claims comparable across connectome projects.

1 Introduction

A measured connectome is a graph of who contacts whom. It is not an operating point, not a dynamical model, and not a behaviour. Three years of open fly-connectome work demonstrate the gap concretely: a complete male central nervous system of about 166,000 neurons is public under CC-BY, yet the projects that run it either supply the controller, supply the semantic mapping from cell groups to behaviour, or supply the reward. The most disciplined attempt to remove the controller reached a negative result: driving the ventral nerve cord through two descending neurons produced a 45.5 Hz population oscillation that a scrambled wiring reproduced as well.

Our starting question is narrower than behaviour. Suppose a network starts small, on a measured wiring, and must add structure as the situations it faces accumulate. What should trigger growth, what should growth wire, and how do we know the growth was worth it?

We answer with a system and a set of measurements. The system, ep4nn, treats growth as a repair of one specific witnessable failure: two situations that the world declares different share the same present input and produce answers that are nearly parallel at the declared readout. The repair is a low-dimensional generator that appends neurons wired from the cells that carry the difference, and a verdict that measures the same collisions again and rolls the growth back when they do not improve.

The main result of this paper is that the repair is blocked by a prerequisite we did not expect to be the binding constraint: on the worm graph, at 60 operating points, the declared readout responds along a single direction. Answers differ in magnitude, not in pattern, so every stage of growth is measuring a code that carries nothing a lesson can attach to. This is the same failure Cadence reports for the fly antennal lobe under one global gain, and it is detectable before any growth is attempted. We therefore state the prerequisite as a rule and report its measurement as the paper’s headline, together with the growth machinery that enforces it.

Contributions:

  1. A growth contract with three gates (prerequisite, evidence, outcome) that makes a refusal to grow an explicit, recorded outcome rather than a silent failure.
  2. A low-dimensional structural generator whose parameters are genes, whose partner choice is driven by the difference between the colliding answers, and whose new neurons must reach the readout.
  3. A state-dependent contraction certificate that certifies a settling budget on a measured connectome where the library’s global bound does not apply.
  4. Measurements of the underlying library on the worm graph and on sparse graphs up to 100,000 neurons, including a negative control result for continuous tracking.
  5. A receipt format for connectome runs, adopted from the fly projects’ practice of separating nominal from executed scale.

2 Related work

Equilibrium learning. Cadence implements settling trained by the contrast of a free and a nudged fixed point, the two-phase form of equilibrium propagation [Scellier and Bengio 2017] and contrastive Hebbian learning [Movellan 1990]. The library’s own documentation states the boundary we rely on: reciprocal learning ties paired weights, and arbitrary directed imports need not satisfy that gradient contract. Equilibrium propagation scales to deep convolutional networks by reducing the bias of its gradient estimator [Laborieux et al. 2021], sits beside implicit differentiation and bilevel formulations [Zucchet and Sacramento 2022], and shares its object with deep equilibrium models [Bai et al. 2019]. Predictive-coding rules approximate backpropagation along arbitrary computation graphs [Millidge et al. 2022; Song et al. 2020].

Complementary memories and replay. Fast episodic storage with slow consolidation is the complementary learning systems proposal [McClelland et al. 1995]. Cadence implements one version: records written once by day, fixed dreams at night taught to slow weights, and the residual written back at dawn. Its own measurements report held-out sign accuracy rising from 0.55 to about 0.76-0.80 on a Connect Four slice, at a night cost of roughly 140-210 s per 1,000 rows for a 4,096-cell store, with the limit that dreams reproduce the store’s errors as faithfully as its regularities.

Fly connectomes. FlyWire published the adult female brain reconstruction of 139,255 neurons [Dorkenwald et al. 2024]; the male central nervous system (brain, optic lobes, ventral nerve cord; about 166,000 neurons) followed under CC-BY. The BANC release used by Cadence’s fly lane carries 150,802 proofread neurons and 1,877,099 connection classes. Because the ventral nerve cord contains the leg and wing motor neurons, the male data made connectome-driven locomotion addressable in a way the brain-only data was not. Open projects in this space differ mainly in what they supply: a hand-wired 850-neuron stand-in with fly cell-type names for a browser demo, a learned consequence model with an application controller, a directly read motor output whose own receipts report that real-time neural control was not demonstrated, and an on-device implementation that reports 10 Hz on a phone using CSR storage and per-group gating. Cadence’s own fly guide reports that at one global gain the antennal lobe ignites, that Kenyon cell codes for two odours reach a cosine of 0.98, and that a gain per cell class brings the code to 4 and 11 percent sparsity with a cosine of 0.32.

Conditional computation. Mixture-of-experts routing, early exit and pruning vary the work per input at the weight level. Dynamic structure methods vary the graph. The distinction we test is the trigger: ep4nn triggers on a witnessable indistinguishability at the declared readout, not on a loss, a router score, or an offline search objective.

3 System

3.1 The four rules

Rule 1, demand. Two situations that the world declares different, share the present input, and produce answers at the declared readout whose absolute cosine exceeds a threshold, constitute a capacity signal. The readout is a declared set of neurons, not the whole state; the whole state almost always separates because it contains the driven cells themselves.

Rule 2, evidence. A single collision is noise. min_evidence collisions must be observed, and min_ticks_between ticks must pass, before any structural change is attempted.

Rule 3, growth. A generator with a handful of genes appends k neurons. Each new neuron draws in-edges and out-edges from a degree prior, prefers partners among the cells that carry the difference between the colliding answers and their one-hop neighbourhood, keeps the graph’s excitatory fraction, adds the reverse edge with probability reciprocal so the equilibrium contrast contract is preserved, and projects into the readout with probability to_readout. The generator is low-dimensional by construction: the number of free parameters is far smaller than the number of edges it can create, which is what distinguishes structural plasticity from implicit weight learning.

Rule 4, verdict. After growth, the same collisions are measured again. Growth is kept only if the collision cosine falls by improve_margin. Otherwise the connectome and the efficacy array are restored from the snapshot taken before the attempt, and the attempt is counted as a failure.

3.2 The prerequisite gate

Before any of the above applies, the answer must move. ep4nn measures readout_spread, the mean over readout cells of the maximum minus minimum activation across a declared set of situations. Below min_response the system records no_response and does not grow. The justification is structural: if the declared answer is constant across situations, no amount of added capacity can reduce a collision, so growth would be the wrong repair and its recorded outcome would carry no information.

3.3 The operating point

A connectome carries contact counts and transmitter signs. It does not carry excitability, and the raw counts of the worm graph give row sums in the hundreds at which the rate model does not settle. ep4nn declares an operating point by row-normalising the signs to row_sum. The library offers two further instruments for the same purpose, calibrate_bias and a gain selected by protocol, and the paper reports measurements for all three.

3.4 A settling budget from a state-dependent certificate

The library’s global contraction bound is a worst-case quantity: it multiplies the largest absolute row sum of the weight matrix by the largest activation slope. On a measured connectome most neurons are inactive at the settled state, so the bound is far larger than the actual contraction. ep4nn measures the certificate the library defines but with the slopes of the current state, \(K = (1-\mathrm{d}t)I + \mathrm{d}t\,W\,\mathrm{diag}(s'(v))\), and reports the spectral radius and a positive weighted witness \(h\) with \(Kh \le \alpha h\). The settling budget follows as \(k \approx \log(\epsilon/\delta)/\log(1/\alpha)\).

4 Implementation

Data. The worm wiring is the openworm c302 A_Full release (302 hermaphrodite neurons, 2,279 chemical synapse classes, 1,084 gap junctions; the gap junctions are imported in both directions). After dropping autapses and merging parallel edges the graph has 3,137 directed synapse classes, 10,133 contacts and a density of 3.44 percent, with 61 sensory, 91 interneuron, 118 motor, 10 command and 22 pharyngeal cells. Soma positions in the source file are used for nothing in this paper.

Library reuse. The network is a cadence.Connectome with cadence.Brain on it, using the library’s rate neuron model, its free and nudged phases, its residual qualification, and its CSR transport. No new learning rule is introduced. The growth layer sits outside the library because Connectome is immutable and because the library forbids growth by design; a growth event rebuilds the connectome, which costs one pass over the edge arrays, and carries the learned efficacy across by regenerating the key order the library sorts by.

Reuse from the fly projects. Two practices are adopted. First, protocols and thresholds are written before the run and the run happens once, following the discipline of the offline fly-body study. Second, every run emits a receipt that separates nominal from executed scale, following the observation that project descriptions and executed graphs differ widely.

Receipt. ep4nn/receipt.py writes a JSON receipt with six fields: dataset and hashes; nominal and executed counts; the dynamical model and its declared parameters; whether the loop is closed and which parts are supplied; the prerequisite measurements; and the cost.

5 Measurements

All measurements are from one Apple Silicon laptop, CPU only, Python 3.13, NumPy 2.5.4 and SciPy 1.18.1, single-threaded, with the released library at version 0.80.0 plus local commits (0b0889e). Probes are in probes/, one file per table.

5.1 Settlement cost and scaling

graph neurons synapses mean in-degree transport per sweep sweeps to 1e-4 settle CSR
worm, measured 302 3,137 10.4 blocked dense 27.2 µs 160 4.89 ms n/a
sparse random 1,000 10,337 10.3 blocked dense 161.4 µs 32 5.29 ms 0.1 MB
sparse random 10,000 103,943 10.4 CSR 193.0 µs 32 6.48 ms 1.3 MB
sparse random 100,000 1,239,921 12.4 CSR 2,185.8 µs 32 77.4 ms 15.3 MB

Cost is about 1.76 ns per synapse, so the control frequency of a settling brain is bounded by \(f \approx 1/(n_{\text{syn}} \times 1.8\,\text{ns} \times \text{sweeps})\). The worm graph needs five times as many sweeps as a random graph of the same size and degree at the same tolerance, which locates the difficulty in the spectrum of the graph rather than in its size. Two library defaults matter at this scale: the default constructors wire at a density of about 30 percent independent of size (measured 29.1, 30.8, 31.7 and 32.3 percent at 152, 280, 536 and 1,048 neurons), so 10,000 neurons would carry about 30 million synapses and settle in about 1.7 s; and the transport path is chosen by a size test rather than by density, so a 3.4 percent dense graph pays a block array 29 times larger than its edge count. On that graph the CSR path is 1.78 times faster end to end (987 µs against 554 µs per settle), while on a 32 percent dense graph the blocked path is faster, which is why the choice must follow density.

5.2 A certificate where the global bound fails

quantity value
global worst-case bound \(q\) 88.0 (292 of 302 rows at or above 1)
local bound, current state 1.186 (1 of 302 rows)
spectral radius of the local \(K\) 0.529
weighted witness \(\alpha\) with \(Kh \le \alpha h\) 0.696, \(h \in [2.89, 11.08]\)
active neurons at the settled state 294 of 302

The global bound gives no certificate at this gain; the state-dependent one does, and it converts a refusal into a bounded settling budget. The same quantity is the precondition for treating the free and nudged contrast as a gradient, which is why it is measured at run time rather than assumed.

5.3 Behaviour of the growth machinery

Two variants were run for 1,200 ticks while the world increased the number of context levels that must be distinguished.

variant kept rolled back refused, no response neurons self-check
without prerequisite gate 0 30 n/a 302 passed
with prerequisite gate 0 0 1,200 302 passed

In both variants the brain never shrank, every synapse kept an efficacy, the readout stayed inside the current graph, and every kept growth would have had to reduce its collisions. In the first variant the collision cosine read 1.000 before and after every attempt, so the verdict layer rolled all 30 attempts back. In the second, the prerequisite measured 0.019 and the system declined to act, recording why.

5.4 The prerequisite scan, a negative result

Sixty operating points were scanned: five row sums, a gain on one cell class (none, interneurons, command neurons), and four gain scales, over nine situations (three single senses and six combinations of an odour with a pain level).

rank row sum gain class scale spread collision cosine sweeps
1 0.5 command 10 0.0777 1.000 224
2 1.0 command 2 0.0559 0.9999 64
3 1.0 none 1 0.0328 0.9998 64
last 2.0 interneurons 10 0.0000 1.000 96

The best configuration moves the readout by 0.078 across situations while leaving every pair of context answers at a cosine of 1.0: the differences that exist lie along one direction, so the answer has rank one with respect to the situations that must be distinguished. Several configurations collapse to an answer that is identical for all nine situations to three decimals, which indicates a fixed point dominated by the recurrent dynamics rather than by the input. We report this as a negative result about the pair (measured worm wiring, rate model at a declared operating point): capacity growth is not the binding constraint, and a growth study on this pair would measure an uninformative object. The remedy is left as future work and has three candidates, in ascending cost: a different declared readout, a dynamics with heterogeneous cells and per-type parameters, or a gain selected by protocol against held-out physiological facts as Cadence does for the fly.

5.5 A negative control result for continuous tracking

One composed brain (140 neurons, 6,028 synapses) was placed in a closed loop on a second-order plant with five force levels, one scalar reward per tick, and 6,000 ticks per arm.

arm mean absolute error last quarter in-band fraction, last quarter dominant action, last quarter
uniform random 1.020 1.033 0.000 uniform
frozen greedy 1.011 1.067 0.467 1.00
learning, sparse reward 0.880 0.855 0.449 0.824
learning, dense reward, seed 5 0.923 0.919 0.209 0.705
learning, dense reward, seed 11 0.856 0.815 0.459 0.843
learning every outcome 1.016 1.007 0.000 0.980

The learned arms reduce mean error by about 13 percent and never beat the frozen baseline on the in-band metric, and the action distribution collapses to a single level. The structural reason is the mismatch between the credit window and the plant: the composed brain’s eligibility span is 12 steps, while the closed-loop time constant of the plant is 50 to 100 steps. This is consistent with the library’s own control-oriented demo, which learns a consequence model and leaves the controller supplied.

6 Discussion

What is shown. The growth contract is implementable on a measured connectome at negligible rebuild cost, its gates fire in the intended order, and the prerequisite measurement is what decides whether structural work is meaningful. Two library-level results are independent of ep4nn: a state-dependent certificate exists where the global bound fails, and a sparse graph is settled at about 1.8 ns per synapse with a per-settle cost that makes 10,000 neurons a real-time-scale object rather than a research curiosity.

What is not shown. No growth has yet been kept. The paper therefore makes no claim about the benefit of structural growth, only about the contract that would measure it and the prerequisite that currently blocks it. We also make no claim that the worm graph is an inappropriate substrate in general; the negative result is specific to the declared readout, the rate model, and the operating points scanned.

Growth is not the same problem as capacity sharing. Specialised small circuits handle single tasks, and Cadence’s own connectome lane reports that a reduced graph matched on population means did not match on member behaviour. Retaining transfer while growing requires shared structure, which is where interference appears. The evidence gate and the outcome gate bound the damage of a wrong growth but do not solve sharing.

Value comes from somewhere. A reward channel needs a scale. A scale supplied by the task caps the system at that task; a scale shaped by selection is endogenous but drifts toward whatever is measured. Both are legitimate designs and they must be declared, because the choice determines which measurements are meaningful. The systems that avoid a human score entirely, by making survival and energy the only currency, are the only ones in this space whose objective is not supplied by a person.

Rule families do not mix. A directed measured connectome does not satisfy the equilibrium contrast gradient contract. Structural growth on directed wiring therefore belongs with a predictive-coding or inference-learning rule, or it must accept that its updates are heuristic. Mixing the two under one set of convergence assumptions is not a defensible default.

7 Reproducibility

Every table above is produced by one file in probes/, and ep4nn/grow.py runs its own assertions. The environment is a virtual environment with NumPy and SciPy installed from a mirror; the library is a local checkout with PYTHONPATH set. The worm connectome is committed with the repository and its SHA-256 recorded. No GPU, no external service and no wallet is involved. Measured numbers depend on the host, so the receipts record the operation counts alongside wall-clock times.

8 Conclusion

A measured connectome gives structure cheaply and dynamics not at all. The useful question for a system that grows is not how many neurons it has but whether the answer it produces separates the situations that must be separated. ep4nn encodes that question as a prerequisite, measures it before it grows, and records a refusal to grow as a result in its own right. On the 302-neuron worm graph the prerequisite fails, and reporting that failure is more useful than adding structure to a code that carries nothing. The contract and the receipt format are the parts we expect to transfer to the next substrate, in particular to connectomes whose motor neurons exist and whose dynamics are heterogeneous.
